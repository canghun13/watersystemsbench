## Deep validation — Top 5

### 1. Monitoring Well Purging & Low-Flow Sampling Operations

**Primary user and workflow.** Environmental field technicians, consultants and site owners repeat `review workplan/SOP → inspect well → calculate applicable purge/equipment volume → place pump and establish low flow → record drawdown/flow → evaluate entered stabilization criteria → document`. This is not a pumping test: it does not estimate transmissivity, storage coefficient, well loss or rehabilitation need.

**Search-intent groups.** `monitoring well purge volume calculator`; `groundwater well purge time`; `low flow groundwater sampling`; `low flow drawdown`; `flow cell volume sampling interval`; `groundwater stabilization criteria`; `groundwater sampling log`; `purge water drum count`.

**Current SERP and competitors.** EPA publishes the low-flow procedure and field-log basis; USGS publishes PAT for analytical in-well flow; UST Contractors provides one traditional volume/drum calculator; public workplans expose printable forms; TabLogs, QNOPY and SampleServe automate stabilization and reporting inside paid field platforms. Current free/account-free browser results do not connect the four planning decisions below.

**Independent tools.** (1) Purge Volume & Time Calculator decides the volume, duration and container count only when the user-selected workplan uses a volume method. (2) Low-Flow Setup Checker compares intake, screen, water levels, drawdown and rate with user-entered project constraints. (3) Equipment Volume & Reading Interval Planner computes tubing/flow-cell displacement and whether an entered reading interval clears the entered exchange count. (4) Stabilization Log Analyzer evaluates the last entered readings against user-entered parameter criteria and preserves a local, printable report. None chooses the method, limits or sampling authorization.

**Repeat use and gap.** Crews rerun all four for different well geometry, tubing, rate, equipment and every monitoring round. Official PDFs are authoritative but not interactive; the free well-volume calculator stops before low-flow setup and stabilization; the integrated alternatives are paid project systems. A free, local-file, vendor-neutral, SI/US workflow has a material opening.

**Existing WSB separation.** Existing well pages size water-supply pumps and compare yield with household demand. The proposed outputs are field purge volume/time, intake/screen geometry, equipment displacement interval and entered-criteria stability—different triggers, decisions and next actions.

**Static implementation, maintenance and safety.** Geometry, unit conversion, elapsed-time integration and user-entered range/relative-percent tests run in Vanilla JS. No map, account, laboratory database, legal table or product catalog is needed. The governing workplan/SOP remains authoritative; contaminated purge water, sample method, bottles, preservation, filtration, QA/QC and disposal stay outside the tools. “Criteria met” never means “sample is representative” or “collection is authorized.”

**Why WSB deserves to exist.** It closes the free gap from well geometry through low-flow evidence without replacing the environmental professional or copying USGS aquifer-analysis software.

### 2. Earthwork Soil Moisture Conditioning & Compaction Control

**Workflow and intents.** Laboratory Proctor points → field moisture → water addition/drying decision → tanker/refill planning → field density acceptance. Intent groups include soil moisture content, water to add to soil, OMC, Proctor curve, MDD, relative compaction and compaction water estimate.

**Competition and gap.** EngineerHub already joins multi-point Proctor data, field dry density, percent compaction and moisture-versus-OMC. Reuven and CivilGeoTools provide detailed field acceptance and plot outputs; other current sites fit the Proctor curve or calculate relative compaction. A water-addition/tanker handoff remains, but the high-value laboratory and acceptance workflow is already free and strong.

**Tool independence and boundary.** Five apparent tools exist, but field moisture, Proctor analysis and percent compaction are already integrated by competitors, while tanker planning inherits the earlier dust/bulk-water logistics pattern. Project specifications, test standards, material change and acceptance remain with the geotechnical team. Static feasibility is strong; competitive opening fails.

### 3. Water-Miscible Metalworking-Fluid Sump Management

**Workflow and intents.** Verify product factor/target → measure Brix → plan initial charge or premixed top-up → log concentration/pH/top-ups → compare concentrate/disposal cost. Searches expose coolant concentration, refractometer factor, mix ratio, top-up, sump maintenance, water hardness and coolant savings.

**Competition and gap.** CNC Market and Zebra already implement refractometer correction and top-up mass balance. Coolant Monitor connects machine logs, alerts, top-ups and reports; several vendor calculators cover recovery ROI. OSHA/NIOSH confirm recurring monitoring and maintenance demand, but the product-neutral free opening is narrower than it first appears.

**Tool independence and boundary.** Charge, correction, log analysis and economics are separable; however, target concentration, factor, water quality, additives, pH action, microbial control and disposal are product/SDS/employer-program decisions. Static calculation is possible, but current calculator plus SaaS coverage fails the opening gate.

### 4. Ice-Rink Resurfacing Water & Energy

**Workflow and intents.** Initial ice build → fill/resurface schedule → flood volume and temperature → freezing/refrigeration load → annual water/energy case → compare demineralized-water scenario. Queries include rink volume, resurfacing gallons/litres, flood temperature, heat load, water use, RO and operating cost.

**Competition and gap.** ASHRAE publishes the flood heat-load method and typical ranges. Inch Calculator covers build and resurfacing volume; current engineering calculators cover refrigeration load; public rink guides explain water-quality and efficiency cases. A connected operator UI is absent, but flood volume, thermal load and annual cost collapse into one repeated mass/energy calculation, and RO production repeats current WSB treatment logic.

**Tool independence and boundary.** Only three strong decisions remain after merging: water audit, transient heat-load/scenario, and season cost. Ice quality, refrigeration capacity and water treatment remain equipment/facility engineering decisions. It fails independent Tool depth despite good static fit.

### 5. Portable Sanitation & Restroom-Trailer Water/Waste Operations

**Workflow and intents.** Attendance/site schedule → unit count → fresh/waste tank balance → refill/pump-out plan → service route → contract cost. Searches expose portable toilet calculator, handwash stations, restroom-trailer fresh tank, waste tank, pump-out, route capacity and profitability.

**Competition and gap.** Many rental calculators already join attendance, duration, alcohol, accessible units and handwash stations. WorkResolved joins route time, waste gallons, disposal and margin. Vendor guidance covers trailer tank constraints, while health departments control potable supply and wastewater disposal. The middle fresh/waste balance is fragmented, but it does not overcome the strong tools on both sides.

**Tool independence and boundary.** Event count, trailer tank, service route and economics are separable. However, accessible-unit ratios, handwash requirements, permitted haulers, food-event water and wastewater rules vary by jurisdiction; user-entered assumptions would make the remaining cluster a thin bridge between strong free calculators. Competitive opening and maintainability fail.

## Final decision

**GO — Monitoring Well Purging & Low-Flow Sampling Operations.**

It is the strongest candidate because it is genuinely new, has eight distinct search-intent groups, supports four independent repeat-use decisions, and fills a current free SERP gap between official PDFs/single calculators and paid field platforms. It remains accurately implementable in the current static architecture, uses only user-entered well/equipment/SOP data and can maintain a clear professional boundary. The implementation does not automate sampling authorization, disposal, laboratory methods, preservation or regulatory compliance.
