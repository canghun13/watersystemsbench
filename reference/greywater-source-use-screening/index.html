Greywater Source & Use Screening Reference

Use this matrix to identify what must be measured or confirmed before planning. It is not a universal list of permitted sources or uses.

Values are provided for transparent preliminary work. Confirm source conditions, product data and project-specific requirements.

Last reviewed: August 8, 2026 · Reference

Source screening

Potential sourcePlanning evidenceCommon concernsGate before reuse
Clothes washerMeasured volume per selected cycle, loads/week, products usedSalts, boron, bleach, lint, hot water, variable event volumeAppliance requirements, approved diversion, subsurface distribution and local source definition
Shower / bathMeasured flow, duration, bath volume and occupancyPathogens, hair, soaps, oils and warm wastewaterAllowed collection, exposure control, treatment/storage rule and maintenance access
Bathroom basinMeasured or observed daily usePersonal-care products, hair and pathogensLocal light-greywater definition and approved end use
Kitchen / dishwasherSeparate flow and composition evidenceFood, fats, oils, grease, detergents and higher organic loadOften treated differently or excluded; obtain explicit local confirmation
Toilet / urinalNot included as greywater by these toolsBlackwater and high pathogen riskUse the applicable wastewater system and authority requirements

End-use screening

Potential useQuantity questionSafety / regulatory question
Subsurface landscape irrigationDoes seasonal plant demand and receiving capacity match each event?Are the source, method, setbacks, crops, exposure controls and season allowed locally?
Laundry-to-landscapeCan whole outlets accept the measured appliance event?Are appliance, valve, labeling, diversion and subsurface rules satisfied?
Toilet flushingDo daily supply, peak demand and treatment/storage capacity match?Requires explicit plumbing, cross-connection, treatment, disinfection and monitoring review; not covered by the sizing tools here.
Surface sprayNot evaluatedExposure and aerosol risk; do not assume it is allowed.
Edible-crop irrigationNot sufficient by itselfCrop-contact and local public-health rules must be checked explicitly.
Potable useOutside scopeThese tools never make wastewater safe to drink or design potable reuse.

Evidence to keep with the plan

  • Authority and rule checked, date and jurisdiction.
  • Measured source volumes and event timing.
  • Allowed end use, season, setbacks and diversion destination.
  • Local ET, effective rainfall, plant factor and hydrozone area.
  • Field infiltration evidence and receiving-basin dimensions.
  • Products used, maintenance schedule and recommissioning observations.
Terminology varies. Greywater and graywater are spelling variants; legal definitions can differ materially. Never infer permission from the label alone.
Reference boundary. Do not convert an indicative value into a universal design, health or legal requirement. Check the cited source, laboratory context, certified claim and applicable jurisdiction.
