Water Quality Parameter Glossary

A parameter has meaning only with its unit, method, sample context, intended use and applicable jurisdiction. Use this glossary to ask the next question, not to declare water safe.

Values are provided for transparent preliminary work. Confirm source conditions, product data and project-specific requirements.

Last reviewed: July 29, 2026 · Reference

Core physical and chemical parameters

ParameterCommon unitWhat it describesTreatment connection and limitation
pHpH unitsHydrogen-ion activity; acidic/basic conditionAffects corrosion, scaling, oxidation and disinfection; pH alone does not define correction chemistry.
Alkalinitymg/L as CaCO₃Acid-neutralizing or buffering capacityImportant for pH stability and dosing; not the same as pH or hardness.
Hardnessmg/L as CaCO₃ or grains/US galPrimarily calcium and magnesiumConnects to softening and scale; does not indicate microorganisms or all dissolved solids.
ConductivityµS/cmAbility to conduct electricity from dissolved ionsUseful trend/salinity indicator; does not identify which ions are present.
Total dissolved solidsmg/LAggregate dissolved material by the stated methodCan connect to RO or source review; one total does not identify health risk or membrane design.
TurbidityNTULight scattering from suspended or colloidal materialCan interfere with filtration/disinfection; it is not a direct pathogen count.
ColorMethod-specific unitsApparent or true color under the stated methodMay involve metals or organics; identify cause before selecting treatment.
OdorQualitative or threshold methodPerceived smell under stated conditionsCan guide testing but cannot diagnose a contaminant or safety.

Biological and operational indicators

Bacteria and indicator organisms
Reported by method-specific presence/absence, counts or most-probable-number units. Indicators and pathogens are not interchangeable; positive or invalid results require the laboratory and health authority’s response.
Dissolved oxygen
Usually mg/L; influences corrosion, oxidation-reduction conditions and biological activity.
Total organic carbon (TOC)
Usually mg/L carbon; an aggregate organic indicator that does not identify individual compounds.
ND and reporting limits
Not detected means below the method/reporting threshold, not proven zero. Always read the qualifier and limit.
No universal table. Drinking-water standards and guidance vary by country, jurisdiction, source and use. Compare results only with the current requirement applicable to the specific project.
Reference boundary. Do not convert an indicative value into a universal design, health or legal requirement. Check the cited source, laboratory context, certified claim and applicable jurisdiction.
